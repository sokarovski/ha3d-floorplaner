# Roofs

Status: **Phases 19.1, 19.2, 19.3 and 19.5 implemented** (owner concept 2026-11-18). Owner decisions are marked as such; everything else here is an implementation convention chosen in 19.1–19.5. 19.3 added step lines, the roof face view, holes and roof windows with the no-fit rules, and attached canopies on any floor; the owner confirmed (2026-11-19, decision 2) that canopies are drawn in floor and room views ([canopies](#attached-canopies)). 19.5 (roof gaps) limits wall-mounted objects and lights by the roof, adds a per-wall Rise to roof, moves canopies in the floor view, and polishes the overlays, the views and the meshes ([19.5](#roof-gaps-195)). 19.4 (dormers) is after the demo. Since 19.2 (house schema version 6) roofs replace the roof cuts of Phase 2 ([roof cut-outs](roof-cutouts.md), superseded); see [migration](#migration-of-roof-cuts).

## The model

A roof is **a flat sheet with fold lines** (owner). It is authored data; its 3D surface is derived and never stored.

| Field (`floors[].roofs[]`, ID prefix `rf_`) | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `points[]` (`rp_`) | Every pullable point: `x`, `y` in the floor's local plan frame (mm) and `heightMm` (≥ 0) above the roof's eave height. |
| `outline[]` | The outline: point IDs, counterclockwise, a simple ring. |
| `lines[]` (`rl_`) | Break lines: `a`, `b` point IDs. 19.3, optional: `kind` (`break`, default, or `step`), and for a step `stepMm` (10–10000 mm, default 500), `raisedSide` (`left` or `right`, seen from `a` toward `b`) and `stepFace` (`side`: the roof's side material, or `wall`: the plain wall paint). |
| `holes[]` (`rh_`, 19.3, optional) | Holes: `name` and a closed ring of plan `points` (3–256) on the roof sheet. |
| `windows[]` (`rw_`, 19.3, optional) | Roof windows: `name`, `templateId`/`templateVersion` (a [roof-window template](object-templates.md#roof-windows)), `positionMm` (its centre on the roof sheet, plan), `widthMm` (level along the face) × `lengthMm` (up the slope), 300–4000 mm, and the template's `minPitchDeg`/`maxPitchDeg` copied in when placed. |
| `eaveHeightMm` | The roof's base above the floor's finished floor (owner: "Eave height"). Default: the floor's tallest wall. Moving it moves the whole roof. |
| `design` | `closed` or `open` (owner: [designs](#designs)). |
| `cutsWalls` | **Cuts walls** (19.2, owner: the top floor's roof): the underside is the height limit of the floor's walls. Default on; off is the **Attached** mode (19.3, [canopies](#attached-canopies)): it cuts nothing. |
| `wallsRise` | **Walls rise** (19.2, owner: "Walls rise to the roof", default on): walls under the roof extend up to the underside where it is above them. Shown while Cuts walls is on. |
| `thicknessMm` | Top to underside, measured vertically (10–1000 mm, default 200). |
| `topMaterial`, `bottomMaterial`, `sideMaterial` | Surface materials (D19). Side: closed design only. |
| `ribHeightMm`, `ribWidthMm`, `ribSpacingMm`, `ribMaterial` (`wood` \| `metal`) | Open design only (defaults 160 × 60 mm, 600 mm apart, wood). |

- **Where:** `BuildingFloor.roofs` is an optional list (absent = none). Add roof adds one cutting roof, on the **top floor** (Add roof refuses others and a second cutting roof); attached canopies (19.3) are further entries with Cuts walls off, on any floor. If a floor ever holds several roofs in Cuts walls mode, the first one cuts (convention).
- **No schema version change in 19.3:** every new field is optional, so version-6 documents stay valid; the HA integration needs a reinstall for its `schemas.json`.
- **The sheet is the underside.** A point's underside is at `eaveHeightMm + heightMm` above the floor; the top is `thicknessMm` higher. 19.2 uses exactly this as the walls' height limit, readable without any derived geometry.
- **Schema version 6** (19.2): the floor's `roofCuts` list is gone and `cutsWalls`/`wallsRise` are required; older documents are migrated on load ([migration](#migration-of-roof-cuts)).
- **Guard rails** (`sheetProblem`, `shared/geometry/roofSheet.ts`, checked by every roof command and by the house rules): references valid, no point below the base (owner: "a point cannot go below the roof's base unless the whole roof is lowered"), the outline simple and counterclockwise with at least three points, break lines and interior points on the roof, at most 400 points.
- **No loose points** (owner): a point that neither the outline nor a line uses is removed with the edit that orphaned it.

## Derived geometry

Pure and framework-free: `shared/geometry/roofSurface.ts` (faces, pieces, folds) and `shared/geometry/roofMesh.ts` (meshes, ribs), memoized by the roof value.

1. **Planar subdivision.** The outline edges and break lines form a plan graph. Edges are split at points lying on them and at any crossing the commands did not split (a derived node, its height along the first line). Lines outside the outline, lines not connected to the outline, and dangling chains do not divide a face (SketchUp: a stray edge does not split a face); the builder shows them dashed as a warning. Faces are the bounded cycles of the graph.
2. **Heights.** Each face is lifted by its points' heights. A triangle, or a face whose points lie on one plane within 1 mm, is one planar piece.
3. **Auto-fold** (owner, SketchUp). A face with four or more points that is not planar is triangulated in plan by ear clipping, always cutting the ear whose new diagonal is shortest in 3D (ties: the lowest vertex index), so a quad folds along its shorter 3D diagonal. Triangles sharing a diagonal merge back while they stay on one plane; the diagonals left between pieces are the **fold lines**, drawn dashed in the roof level. Drawing a break line replaces a fold. Deterministic.
4. **Step lines** (19.3, [step lines](#step-lines)): around every node on a step line, the faces meeting there are taken in angular order; the face on the raised side gets the step at that corner, and so does every face reached from it across ordinary lines until another step or the outline's outside. Steps are no break lines for ribs; each stretch keeps its low and raised heights (`steps`), and the outline's edges carry the heights of the face inside them (`rim`).
5. **Meshes** (floor frame): each piece's triangles at the underside (normal down) and `thicknessMm` above (normal up), and one vertical edge band along the outline (at the faces' heights), a closed solid whose volume is the outline area × thickness (less the holes). **Physical UVs** for tiles: on a sloped piece u runs level along the contour and v up the slope's surface, so tile rows run along the eaves; flat pieces use plan x/y; the edge band runs u along the outline. 19.3: holes and roof windows are cut out of every piece (the piece's region less the cut, triangulated again), lined with a vertical band facing into the hole, and clip the ribs; each step stretch gets its vertical faces.

## Designs

| | Closed | Open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mesh | top, underside, edge | the same deck (its edge in the top material) + ribs below |
| Materials | top, bottom, side | top (covering), bottom (underside); ribs plain timber or light metal |

**Ribs** (owner algorithm): every piece is a plane with a downhill direction; its ribs run downhill at the spacing, the set centred across the piece, each clipped to the piece (a rib shorter than its width is dropped); a flat piece's ribs run parallel to its longest boundary edge. Every break line and every fold line gets a rib along it; since 19.5 the sides of such a rib meet the underside of the face each lies under, so a valley rib closes against both slopes (no gap) and a ridge rib does not poke into the deck (a step line's rib keeps the line's height). A rib is a sheared box hanging `ribHeightMm` below the underside, `ribWidthMm` wide, its sides vertical and its bottom parallel to the plane. Ribs of neighbouring pieces do not line up at seams (accepted by the owner: seen only from below).

**Materials, v1** (owner): top terracotta tiles, slate tiles, or **Aluminium roofing** (the built-in `builtinimg_aluminium`, a light standing-seam metal, 2000 mm repeat, metallic 0.35; [materials and objects](materials-and-objects.md#1-materials-and-image-textures)); underside a plain colour or a wood. New roofs: Terracotta tiles on top, Ash planks underneath, Plain white sides. No gutters, chimneys or fascia boards, ever (owner decision 3).

## Step lines

Phase 19.3 (owner: "a line whose two sides have their own heights; the roof makes a vertical face along it, owned by the roof, with the roof's side material (or a chosen wall material)").

- A break line becomes a step line in its properties (schema-driven): **Kind** Step line, **Step height**, **Raised side** (left or right, seen from its first point toward its second; convention, since a line has no other natural side) and **Step face** (Roof sides or Wall).
- **Two heights along the line:** the line's own points keep their heights for the low side; the raised side is `stepMm` higher along it. The rest of each side keeps its own points' heights (the "pull points" model): a flat sheet with one step makes the raised side slope back down to its eave (a sawtooth); pull its far points up for a level upper part. A step from the outline to the outline keeps its height; one ending inside the roof dies out there (both sides meet at that end, a tapering step face).
- **The vertical face:** above the low side's top up to the raised side's top (facing the low side) and under the raised side from the low side's underside up to its own (facing the raised side), in the roof's side material (closed; the covering for open) or the plain wall paint.
- Splitting a step line (a crossing line) keeps both halves steps. The walls' roof limit follows each side (per piece); a wall crossing a step has its two heights and, since 19.5, a **vertical wall face at the step** across its thickness, from the low side's underside up to the raised side's (cap material, like the step face where the outline crosses a wall; `roofClip.ts` `stepFaces`).
- **Windows in step faces** (owner: "later, as roof-hosted openings") are not built: a step face is plain.

## The roof face view

Phase 19.3 (owner 2026-11-18): inside the roof's level, a **double-click on a flat face** opens its face view, one level deeper ("Editing House › Roof › Face"; the header says "Roof face"). The camera looks straight at that slope (orbit locked, like a wall's face view; a flat face is seen from just short of straight down); **Done, Escape, or a double-click off the roof** go back to the roof's level (Top view, fitted), a double-click on another face of the roof opens that one. Faces are derived from the break lines and can change, so the level keeps the **plan point** that was double-clicked, not a face index: the face under it is the one shown.

- **Face coordinates:** u runs level along the slope's contour, v up the slope's surface (mm, the tile frame of the slope); the face view converts them to the roof sheet (plan) by projecting vertically, so everything drawn is stored in plan.
- **Toolbox:** Select (Space) and Move (M) pick and move holes and roof windows; **Rectangle (R), Circle (C), Polygon (P)** draw a **hole** (the shared shape tools, in face coordinates, snapping to the face's outline and the other holes and windows; a circle is 24 points); **Roof window (W)** opens the roof-window picker and the chosen template lands centred where the face was clicked; Pan.
- **Move** (D16): grab a hole or window (or the selection), move it on the slope; a second click, Enter after a typed distance, or the release of a drag commits one undo step; Shift keeps it level or straight up the slope; Escape cancels. The move is converted to a plan offset on the face's plane. In the roof's Top view the same items are picked (after points and lines) and moved on the plan.

## Holes and roof windows

Phase 19.3 (owner: "a closed shape drawn on a roof face, like floor and wall openings"; skylights). Both live on the **roof sheet** (plan), never in 3D (owner): their height always comes from the surface under them, so they can never hang in thin air, and after a line edit they stay where they were, on whichever face is under them (`shared/geometry/roofOpenings.ts`).

- **A hole** cuts the roof along its outline projected vertically onto the faces under it: top, underside, the lining band between them (side material) and the ribs. It may span several faces (it is cut on each).
- **A roof window** is a [roof-window template](object-templates.md#roof-windows) (D17 glTF, Wallable hosted by a roof) on the flat piece under its centre, oriented to that piece's plane (template x level along the slope, y up it, z the outward normal), its lining fitting the slab's thickness across the slope; it cuts its rectangle (width × length on the slope) like a hole. Placed from the face view's Roof window tool or the roof row's **Roof window…** (+ menu: on the largest face whose pitch suits the template, else the largest). Its properties are Name, Width × Length (schema-driven).
- **When one no longer fits** (owner, phase-19.md; never deleted or moved automatically, an issue in the tree, the selection details and the agent's get_geometry; dashed in the warning tone in the roof's level):
  - on one face → drawn normally, following the new slope;
  - a hole entirely outside the outline after an outline edit → cuts nothing ("Hole 1 is outside the roof"); one partly outside is cut where it is over the roof;
  - a roof window outside (or partly outside) the outline → not drawn, cuts nothing;
  - a roof window crossed by a break or fold line → its hole is cut on both faces, the window is not drawn (it cannot sit on two slopes);
  - a slope outside the window template's pitch limits → not drawn, cuts nothing (convention: no open hole is left).
  The user fixes it by selecting it (issue or tree row, under the roof) and moving it in the face view or the Top view.
- **Walls:** a hole or roof window **does not change the walls' roof limit** (decision 19.3): the limit is the underside the roof would have there, so walls under a skylight keep their height and the window limit of doors and windows is unchanged.
- Delete removes them (tree trash or Delete); they are listed under their roof's tree row.

## Attached canopies

Phase 19.3 (owner: "Attached (canopies, e.g. over a balcony): placed like an object at any height, cuts nothing").

- **Add canopy** is in every building floor's + menu and every room's and yard's + menu: a flat 120 mm canopy (aluminium on top, white underneath, closed design) over the area's bounding box when it is at most 6 m each way, otherwise 3000 × 2500 mm at its centre (at the floor's centre from a floor's menu), at 2400 mm, or on a room's tallest wall when that is higher. It is a roof with **Cuts walls off** (`cutsWalls: false`, `wallsRise: false`): it limits no wall, raises no wall, flags no item above it.
- **Moving it like an object:** (19.5, owner 2026-11-19) select it in the floor or room view (a click on it) and use **Move (M)** without entering it: the whole canopy moves on the plan (↑ locks blue: its height; → red, ← green; Shift the dominant axis), with typed values and the click slop, live, one undo step (`moveRoofCommand`: every point, its holes and roof windows by the offset, the eave by ↑; never below the floor); Escape cancels. Inside its level (double-click), Move with nothing of it selected does the same (holes and windows now move with it there too); its points, lines, step lines, holes and windows edit as on any roof. Leaving a canopy's level returns to its floor with it selected (a cutting roof returns to the whole house).
- **Drawn in floor views (owner decision 2, 2026-11-19):** floor views never draw *the roof* (owner decision 4); a canopy is part of its floor (a canopy over a terrace belongs to that terrace), so floor and room views, in the builder and in HA, draw a floor's **attached** roofs (`attachedRoofsOnly`, `src/renderer/roofLayer.ts`) and never its cutting roof. In the builder floor view a canopy picks (when nothing of the floor is in front of it), selects (its outline highlights) and opens with a double-click. **In HA** (owner): taps pass through a canopy to the floor below (the canopy is not in the pick list: a tap on it reaches the room, light or object under it), and the floor's framing (camera fit, shadow fit, focus) **ignores canopies**: HA frames the rooms it controls, and a canopy usually lies over or next to them (a terrace canopy outside the rooms may be cut at the view's edge; implementation convention).

## Adding a roof

**Add roof** is in the top floor's + menu (once). It adds a **flat** roof whose outline is the floor's bounding box (its rooms with their walls, yards left out) grown by **400 mm on every side** (owner decision 2: there is no overhang setting; the overhang is simply the outline drawn larger than the walls), at the eave height of the floor's tallest wall, and selects it in the whole house. A floor without rooms gets no roof (a notice says so).

## Where roofs are drawn

Only in the **whole-house views** (the builder's whole-house overview and the HA viewer's whole house) and while **editing the roof**; floor and room views never draw it (owner decision 4: opening a floor in HA shows it without its roof). Attached canopies (19.3) are drawn in their floor's views too (owner decision 2, 2026-11-19; [canopies](#attached-canopies)).

**Shadow-only ceilings under the roof** (19.3 fix): the invisible shadow-casting ceiling every room gets at its tallest authored wall (Phase 15.5) followed that flat height even where the roof's underside is lower (the demo attic: eave 720 mm, walls 2700 mm), so it poked up through the slopes and shaded the roof's own top, a soft darker band along the eaves in both whole-house views (identical with Walls rise on or off, since it read the authored heights). Under a cutting roof the ceiling now follows min(ceiling height, underside) per roof piece (`shared/geometry/roofCeiling.ts`), so it never reaches above the underside; outside the roof it is flat as before. No shadow-map or lighting value changed. A one-floor house with a roof therefore has the builder's whole-house level too (`hasHouseOverview`). In the whole house a roof casts and receives shadows, merges with its floor in the static batch (its own bucket, so picks still name it), and the framing reaches its top and overhang. See [renderer](../../architecture/renderer.md#roofs).

## The roof editing level

- **Enter:** double-click the roof in the whole-house overview, or double-click / Enter its tree row (under the top floor). The level opens in **Top** view, the roof drawn over its floor, its outline, break lines, folds and points overlaid; the tree is locked to the roof's row. Header: "Editing House › Roof".
- **Leave** (16.13 rules): Done, Escape (after cancelling, Select and clearing the selection), the House link, or a double-click off the roof: back to the whole house with the roof selected. Since 19.5 the view (3D or Top) the user had before entering returns (`viewModeBeforeRoof`, session-only); the level itself always opens in Top.
- **Toolbox:** Select (Space), Move (M), Line (L) │ Rectangle (R), Circle (C), Polygon (P) │ Orbit, Pan. A double-click on one of its faces opens the [roof face view](#the-roof-face-view) (19.3).
- **Select** picks points and break lines (screen-space: 10 px for points, 6 px for lines; Shift adds or removes). Alt-click on an outline edge inserts a point there (at the edge's height); Alt-click on a point deletes it. Delete removes selected points and lines (an outline keeps three points). A selected point shows its **Height** in the properties panel (typed, schema-driven); a line shows no fields.
- **Move** (D16 click–lock–type–commit, SketchUp's Move): grabs the selection (a line pulls its two ends) or what is under the pointer; pressed on the roof's surface with nothing of it selected it moves the whole roof (↑: its eave height). The roof previews live; a second click, Enter after a typed distance, or the release of a drag commits **one undo step**; Escape cancels. **↑ locks blue** (pull up and down; in Top view the pointer's screen movement reads as height), → red, ← green (the same key again or ↓ frees it); Shift locks the dominant red/green axis; Ctrl moves without snapping. Plan moves edit the outline and line ends and snap to the roof's points and edges and the floor's walls; blue pulls snap to other points' heights ("Height of a point"). A pull down **stops at the eave height** (guard rail); a plan move that would cross the outline or leave the roof is refused (shown in red).
- **Line** draws break lines: click a start and an end, or type a length and Enter. Ends snap to roof points, the outline and other lines; an end on the outline becomes an outline point, an end on a line splits it, and a line crossing another is **split at one shared point** (owner). New points take the roof's current height there, so drawing never changes the shape. The line continues from its end (SketchUp) until it lands on an existing point, edge or line, or Escape.
- **Rectangle, Circle, Polygon** are the shared shape tools; in the roof they draw a **new outline** (a flat sheet; the break lines go, Undo restores them; a notice says so). A circle becomes 24 points (convention: each a pullable point).
- Examples (owner): one middle line pulled up is a gable; four lines from the corners to a centre point pulled up a hip; lines from a circle's points to its centre a cone; outer points pulled make a shed or different eave heights.

## Properties

The roof's panel is schema-driven (D18, `Roof` in `shared/domain/schemas/roof.ts`): General (Name, Design, Cuts walls and Walls rise (19.2, Yes/No toggles; Walls rise shown while it cuts), Eave height, Thickness), Materials (Top, Underside, Sides for closed), Ribs (height × width, spacing, Wood/Metal; open only, `visibleWhen`). 19.3: a line shows Kind, and for a step Step height, Raised side and Step face; a hole its Name; a roof window Name and Width × Length.

## The roof cuts walls

Phase 19.2 (owner: Cuts walls, Walls rise to the roof). The underside over any plan point is `eaveHeightMm + roofHeightAt(roofSurface(roof), point)`, with no derived walls needed (`shared/geometry/roofLimit.ts`: `floorRoofLimit`, `roofUndersideAt`).

- **Per piece, not half-spaces.** Each planar piece of the roof (faces and auto-fold pieces) is a plane limited to its own plan region. The pieces partition the outline and share their break and fold lines, so the limit is continuous inside the outline; **outside the outline there is no limit** and walls keep their height.
- **Clipping** (RealAllWalls, `roofClip.ts`, `wallMesh.ts`): while the walls are extruded, every vertical face is split where it crosses a piece boundary and each stretch is trimmed to `min(top, underside)` of the piece over it (probed 0.5 mm inside the wall, so a face lying on the outline counts as covered); every height slice gets sloped caps (cap material) per piece where `bottom < underside ≤ top`; horizontal caps, sills and lintel undersides survive only where the underside is above them; where the outline runs through the walls, the uncovered part shows a vertical **step face** (cap material) from the underside up. Openings are clipped with the wall. Walls follow the roof along their length and across their thickness, exactly as the roof cuts did, but per piece region.
- **Walls rise to the roof** (default on): before slicing, each wall band under the roof whose top is below the highest underside over it is raised to that height, and the clipping then trims it to the underside, so gable ends fill to the ridge and eave walls follow the slope. A band crossing the outline is split there and only its covered part rises. Off, walls are only clipped (as the roof cuts did).
- **Interior walls rise too** (decision 19.2, SketchUp: walls meet the roof): every wall of the floor under the roof rises, partitions included, and so do low walls (a 1 m parapet under the roof becomes a full wall). **Rise to roof** (19.5, owner): a wall's own switch (schema-driven, Size group; absent = on, following the roof's Walls rise) opts one wall out: it keeps its authored height, still clipped where the underside is lower ([wall properties](../object-models.md#wall)). Walls **outside the outline** keep their authored height and are not clipped; a wall partly under it rises (or is clipped) only where covered.
- **Authored heights stay.** `wall.heightMm` is never changed: the risen height is derived. Turning the roof's Cuts walls off, deleting the roof, or lowering it gives the authored walls back. Add floor's suggested elevation and the overhead reference of pendants still read the authored heights.
- **Openings in risen walls:** an opening fits its wall up to the risen height (a gable window above the authored top is cut); the window limit below keeps it under the roof.
- **Picking:** `wallsAtPoint` ignores heights above the underside.
- **Wall face view** (19.5, owner): the face view of a wall under the roof frames its **risen (or clipped) shape**, a gable end's triangle up to the ridge, not only the authored rectangle: the face's top along its length (`wallFaceTopProfile`, `roofItems.ts`) sets the frame's height, the face's outline the shape tools snap to, and a snap height at the peak.

## Window limit

Phase 19.2 (owner). A door or window goes through its wall, so its top must stay under the underside along its whole width **on both faces** of the wall (`shared/geometry/roofItems.ts`: `wallItemTopLimitMm` reads the face lines 0.5 mm inside the wall over the opening's span).

- **Placing** an opening from the picker: under a roof it is **lowered** until its top is under the underside (a notice says so), never below the floor; one too tall for the space there is **refused** with a message.
- **Moving** it along its wall (the Move cross, click–lock–commit): a place where its top would be above the roof is refused; the opening stays at its last place under the roof and the cross turns red, with a hint.
- **Resizing** it (stretch handles): the top **stops at the underside** over the item (snapped down; the underside is also a height snap), and a wider box whose top would reach above the roof is refused. The limit is drawn across the box as a dashed warning line while stretching.
- **The agent's place_opening** refuses an opening above the roof.
- **Wall-mounted objects and lights** (19.5, owner: "dragging wall-mounted items (shelves, lamps) stops under the roof like doors and windows"): a wall-hosted object is checked along its width on the face it hangs on, a wall-mounted light (Phase 17.3) at its point on its face (probed just inside the wall). Sliding one along its wall (Nudge, the Move cross) refuses places above the underside (it stays at its last place, the cross turns red, a hint says why); resizing a wall object stops at the underside with the dashed limit line (as for openings); a wall-mounted light's Raise stops at the underside over it, drawn as a dashed limit line, and its Move refuses places where it would be above it; placing a wall-mounted object from the picker lowers it under the roof (a notice says so) or refuses one too tall (`lightTopLimitMm`, `wallItemTopLimitMm` with the object's face, `roofItems.ts`). Free lights and floor objects are not limited (issues only).
- Typed values in the properties panel are not refused; an opening, object or light above the roof is then an issue.
- **Issues** (`itemsAboveRoof`): doors and windows (along their width, both faces), wall-mounted objects (along their width on the face they hang on), floor objects (stairs: their rise), overhead objects (from the area's tallest authored wall less their elevation) and lights (at their plan point) whose top is above the underside are flagged in the tree and the selection details ("This reaches above the roof"), and listed by the agent's get_geometry (`aboveRoof`). They are never clipped or moved by a roof edit.

## Migration of roof cuts

Schema version 5 → 6 (Phase 19.2, `shared/domain/house/migrateV5RoofCuts.ts`), once on load; the roof-cut tool, its tree row, its profile popup and its issues are gone.

- Every floor loses `roofCuts`; every existing roof gains `cutsWalls` and `wallsRise` (both on).
- A floor that **already has a roof** just drops its cuts (the demo attic carried both, its roof's underside on the cuts' planes).
- A floor with active, valid cuts and **no roof** gets one roof reproducing them: the outline is the floor's bounding box (rooms with their walls, as Add roof) grown by Add roof's 400 mm, or the bounding box itself when the overhang would bring the envelope below the floor; break lines divide it into the cells of the cuts' **lower envelope** (each cell is where one cut is lowest, a convex polygon), every point's height is the envelope there, the eave the lowest point. The envelope is the same function over any outline containing the walls, so **the walls keep exactly the heights the cuts gave them**; for that the migrated roof has **Walls rise off** (the cuts only removed). Closed, Add roof's materials, ID `rf_` + the floor ID's suffix (deterministic).
- Inactive cuts and vertical profiles had no effect and are dropped. A cut that went below the floor is clamped to the floor (the only case where the result can differ).
